# Fictional Sample — Evidence Passport Quick Audit

> Demonstration only. All names, sources, measurements, and numbers below are invented. This is not evidence about a real product.

## Fictional project: GreenSeat

GreenSeat is a hypothetical public bench concept intended to reduce hot contact surfaces while using recycled material.

### Supplied source summaries

- **S1 — Founder note:** proposes a recycled-plastic bench with a pale mineral contact surface and claims it “feels at least 4°C cooler.”
- **S2 — Supplier sheet:** states that the structural polymer contains 40% recycled material; no batch certificate or outdoor-aging data is supplied.
- **S3 — Prototype log:** reports one midday comparison: pale prototype 42.3°C versus black reference 44.4°C. Calibration, shade, wind, geometry, and raw readings are absent.

## Readable project brief

**Potential benefit.** A cooler public contact surface could improve comfort during hot periods while the structural core may reduce virgin-material use.

**Distinct mechanism.** The concept separates a pale, solar-reflective contact surface from a recycled structural core. Those two contributions must be tested separately.

**Strongest reported result.** S3 reports a 2.1°C difference in one comparison. This is not independently verified and does not establish the 4°C comfort claim, durability, or causation.

**Central hypothesis.** Under matched sunlight, wind, geometry, and starting temperature, the pale surface produces a useful and repeatable temperature reduction without unacceptable glare, wear, cost, or environmental burden.

**Cheapest reversible test.** Compare three same-size coupons: (A) black reference, (B) pale coating on an ordinary substrate, and (C) the same pale coating on the proposed recycled core. Randomise positions; record shade, wind, ambient temperature, surface temperature, and time every five minutes across at least three different sunny periods, using the same operator and thermometer.

**Stop or redesign if:**

- coupon C does not outperform the pale ordinary-substrate control in a relevant way;
- glare, wear, delamination, or touch safety is unacceptable;
- the material and cost increase cannot be justified by the measured benefit; or
- the effect cannot be reproduced across sessions.

## Failure routes and alternatives

- **Primary failure mode:** surface colour or unequal exposure explains the entire difference; the recycled core adds no useful effect.
- **Alternative explanations:** shade, wind, thermometer error, sensor calibration, geometry, and thermal mass.
- **Missing dependency:** the original raw log plus exact material, coating, and geometry specifications.

## Decision

`PREPARED — NOT VALIDATED`

Run only the matched coupon test next. Do not yet claim a 4°C reduction, verified recycled content, improved comfort, or environmental benefit.

## What the audit changed

The audit turned one promotional statement into: one weak reported observation, several explicit hypotheses, a test that isolates coating from core material, early falsifiers, and a safer set of claims. That is the intended value of the Quick Audit.
