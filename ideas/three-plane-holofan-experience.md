## Source-supported design intent

Remco's intended first experience is:

- a half-enclosing arrangement of linked holofans;
- preferably three transparent or partly transparent display planes instead of one;
- a full 3–5 minute short experience;
- a conversion method for ordinary or animated film material;
- subject-centred holographic moments, because an entire conventional background is unlikely to survive conversion equally well;
- a repeatable “formula” that recognizes scenes where depth, enclosure or boundary crossing can become spectacular.

This is preserved as design intent, not as proof that available hardware can deliver it.

## Conventional baseline

A spinning LED fan is a planar persistence-of-vision display. Multiple planes may provide depth ordering and limited parallax cues, but can also multiply brightness loss, occlusion errors, ghosting, synchronization error, moiré/aliasing, heat, noise and mechanical risk. “Hologram” is therefore a presentation label here, not a claim of free-space volumetric light.

## WijSAmen difference

The strongest candidate is not simply adding more fans. It is a **source-to-depth conversion grammar** that decides which image elements belong on which plane and when an element should move between planes.

Candidate high-value scene events:

1. a subject approaches or crosses the display boundary;
2. an object or camera move circles a single subject;
3. a foreground object reveals or occludes a subject;
4. particles, cloth, wings, water or light trails bridge planes;
5. a subject is handed off from rear to middle to front plane;
6. the conventional background fades into a restrained atmospheric shell while only selected bodies gain depth.

The formula must be able to abstain when a scene has no reliable subject separation or depth advantage.

## Potential benefit

A successful conversion grammar could turn short existing animation or licensed film fragments into an enclosing experience without requiring every frame to become a full volumetric scene. The same grammar could later serve purpose-built animation, live performance and accessible previsualization.

## Key unknowns

- whether three planes add perceived enclosure beyond one well-composed plane;
- how much transparency, brightness and contrast survive through front layers;
- acceptable plane spacing and viewer zone;
- synchronization tolerance across rotating displays;
- whether automatic segmentation and depth estimation are reliable enough;
- which scene classes consistently benefit;
- whether the physical safety envelope still permits a half-enclosing layout.

## Cheapest reversible test

Build a software-only **three-plane animatic** before buying or spinning hardware:

1. select one owned, public-domain or explicitly licensed 20–30 second animation fragment;
2. produce a one-plane baseline and a three-plane version from identical source frames;
3. export synchronized transparent rear, middle and front videos;
4. simulate at least three viewer positions and deliberately inject timing, brightness and depth-order errors;
5. score perceived enclosure, depth-order mistakes, subject readability, ghosting and conversion labour;
6. repeat with one scene the formula rejects.

Advance only if the three-plane version provides a repeatable advantage that survives the error tests.

## Disconfirming evidence

Park or redesign if:

- one plane is equally convincing;
- the effect works only from one exact viewpoint;
- front layers erase rear content;
- timing tolerance is tighter than practical hardware can maintain;
- most scenes require extensive manual rotoscoping;
- the experience depends on unsafe proximity, intolerable flicker/noise or misleading “true hologram” claims.

## Safety-by-design and misuse boundary

No hardware purchase or public demonstration is authorized by this note.

Any physical prototype must have guarded rotating parts, containment, stand-off distance, stable mounting, thermal and electrical protection, emergency stop, startup self-test and a documented crowd boundary. Content testing must include photosensitivity/flicker, disorientation and accessibility checks.

The conversion pipeline must preserve source provenance, rights status, transformation settings and an abstention/error log. It must not be used for deceptive impersonation, unauthorized copyrighted exhibition, covert surveillance or deliberately confusing a public audience about what is real.
