## Purpose

Preserve the unusual mirror/material question without promoting folklore, historical speculation, visual impressions, or assistant confidence into evidence.

MirrorBench compares safe, documented layered surfaces for measurable optical and electromechanical behavior. It does not authorize physical execution and it makes no propulsion, energy-generation, neurological, or historical-causation claim.

## Evidence boundary

### Source-supported baseline

Optical response can vary with:

- material optical constants;
- layer order and thickness;
- surface roughness and oxidation;
- substrate absorption;
- wavelength and incidence angle;
- polarization and scatter;
- mechanical vibration and temperature.

Conductive layers may also show ordinary capacitive-shielding or eddy-current behavior under appropriate conditions.

### WijSAmen difference

Treat mirror-like structures as multifunctional material stacks rather than only as image reflectors. Candidate value survives only if a reproducible measurement differs from a matched conventional reference and remains after confounds are controlled.

### Explicitly unverified

- historical dismantling or folklore caused by a special mirror composition;
- “mysterious energy” or hidden-field explanations;
- net propulsion or lift without an external momentum-exchange path;
- neurological significance inferred from appearance alone.

## Hard safety exclusions

- No mercury, mercury amalgam, mercury–aluminium contact, or unknown liquid-metal system.
- Do not open, abrade, scratch, heat, drill, dissolve, or chemically test antique or unknown mirrors.
- Do not dismantle heritage objects.
- Unknown historical coatings require a conservator or qualified non-destructive XRF service.
- Use only low-power visible illumination and commercially known, inert materials.
- No human sensory, neurological, therapeutic, or VR exposure experiment is part of MirrorBench.

## Safe candidate matrix

Possible documented coupons:

- clear glass;
- green glass;
- commercial household mirror;
- aluminium foil;
- copper foil;
- brass;
- stainless steel;
- inert coloured optical film;
- a supplier-documented commercial coating.

Each coupon record must include provenance, material, layer order, known thickness, oxidation state, dimensions, and an uncertainty note.

## Comparator discipline

Every candidate is paired with the closest conventional comparator under the same:

- illumination source and distance;
- angle jig and orientation;
- camera or sensor settings;
- temperature window;
- vibration condition;
- background and reference surface.

A phone camera is comparative only; it is not calibrated spectroscopy.

## Evidence ladder

1. Literature and supplier-data check.
2. Safe coupon design.
3. Fixed-geometry baseline capture.
4. Blinded or randomized candidate/comparator ordering.
5. Repeatability across sessions.
6. Independent replication.
7. Only then: a bounded concept note for qualified collaboration.

No step may be skipped because a visual effect looks striking.

## Success and failure

**Success:** one preregistered optical or vibration subclaim is reproducible, exceeds the frozen measurement threshold, survives comparator controls, and is independently replicated.

**Failure:** the candidate does not differ reproducibly from known material optics, the signal disappears under controls, or the setup cannot bound uncertainty. Failure preserves any useful fixture, calibration, or materials knowledge.
