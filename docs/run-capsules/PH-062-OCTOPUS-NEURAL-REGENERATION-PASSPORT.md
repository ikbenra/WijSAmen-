## Source-supported core

Primary studies support a bounded claim: *Octopus vulgaris* can regenerate a completely transected pallial nerve and recover pathway functions over weeks to months. The repair sequence includes early degeneration, hemocyte activity, scar/connective-tissue formation, axon regrowth across a gap, target re-entry and functional recovery. Acute arm wounds also show rapid contraction, epithelial migration and a temporary cellular plug.

Important contradiction boundary:

- recovery is pathway-, species- and experiment-specific;
- regenerated architecture can remain disordered;
- older studies reported variable or incomplete recovery;
- current evidence does not identify one transferable molecular switch;
- no direct inference to mammalian central nervous-system repair, supplements, stimulation or self-treatment is valid.
